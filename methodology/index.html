Our methodology

The SAT is its own world. We teach your teen its language.

The SAT has its own language: advanced vocabulary, hyper-professional writing, and math questions asked in deliberately unusual ways. It’s intimidating, and even motivated students struggle to stay with something that feels that foreign. Our job is to make it familiar — and then make it work for your teen.

How we teach

Six principles behind every lesson.

Built by Educational Director Alex Torres from 17 years of teaching the SAT, and used in every class, office hour, and homework assignment.

  1. 01

    Put the effort where the points are

    SAT passages are packed with extreme specifics. We teach students to tell those apart from the structural cues in the writing — the signals that actually carry value for test takers — so their energy goes to the things that matter most.

  2. 02

    Breeze through the passage. Scrutinize the answers.

    Most students pour their effort into reading the passage, then pick an answer quickly. We flip that. Students move briskly through the text, setting aside names, dates, and other concrete details unless they turn out to matter, and slow down on the answer choices — where a single word is often the difference between right and wrong. It feels like less effort, and it yields far more.

  3. 03

    Judgment, not just rules

    No one succeeds on the SAT by staying big-picture all the time, or by obsessing over every detail. The real skill is knowing which mode a question calls for. It’s the same judgment that separates successful college students — and we train it every week.

  4. 04

    Learn the SAT’s language

    Today’s SAT leans on algebra, basic geometry, and shorter passages. To separate students, test writers layer in difficulty another way: unconventional wording, common words used in unusual ways (“disrupt” or “consequence” with a positive meaning), and vocabulary that’s common in professional writing but outside most teens’ everyday speech. Some “pet words” (like engender) and “pet math concepts” (the unit circle, the altitude to the hypotenuse of a right triangle) show up again and again. We know what weighs most — so your teen does too.

  5. 05

    Desmos: a tool, not a shortcut

    Students hear that mastering the built-in Desmos graphing calculator is the secret to the Math section. We find those claims overblown. Desmos can save real time on certain questions — and become a trap on others. We teach students when it helps, and when to skip it.

  6. 06

    Always studying the newest test

    Since the SAT went digital, we’ve been among the first to analyze it and translate it for students and parents. We still learn something new every week, and it goes straight into how we teach.
